# Phonology

> Parent: [[Ortatürk]]

Upstream Ortatürk deliberately allows pronunciation variation ("известная вариативность произношения возможна и вполне допустима"), since many Turkic languages lack pronunciation dictionaries.

## Consonants

| | Labial | Dental/Alv. | Postalv./Palatal | Velar | Back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nasal | m | n | | ŋ | |
| Voiceless plosive | p | t | tʃ | k | q |
| Voiced plosive | b | d | dʒ | ɡ | |
| Voiceless fricative | f | s | ʃ | x~χ | h~ħ |
| Voiced fricative | v~w | z | ʒ | ɣ~ʁ | |
| Approximant | | l | r | j | |

Notes on the upstream correspondences:

- Upstream k and g are explicitly *soft* (palatalized kʲ, gʲ); q and ğ are their back counterparts — the consonant side of vowel harmony.
- Upstream merges our խ and հ into a single letter h, pronounced [x~h]. We keep both letters: խ continues upstream h, while հ is available for loans with a true [h].
- Upstream v is absent from the phonetics list, but w appears in example words (kähwe "coffee"); վ covers the v~w range.
- ŋ is upstream ñ.

## Vowels

Nine vowels, in the classic Turkic front/back × round harmony grid:

| | Front | Back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High unrounded | i | ɯ |
| High rounded | y | u |
| Low unrounded | e · ä(æ) | a |
| Low rounded | ø | o |

Upstream e is [e] (non-jotated) in the first syllable of a root, [ɛ] in later syllables. The ninth vowel ä /æ/ (as in English *cat*) is the Crimean Tatar/Uyghur-style front low vowel.
