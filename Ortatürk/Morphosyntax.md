# Morphosyntax

> Parent: [[Ortatürk]]

Ortatürk is agglutinative with regular suffixation and consistent SOV order. Grammatical categories are averaged the same way the lexicon is — by double majority across the Turkic languages.

## Verbs

**Infinitive:** -maq/-mek (մաք/մեք), or -ırğa/-irge/-urğa/-ürge/-arğa/-erge/-rğa/-rge. The infinitive carries no perfective/imperfective aspect meaning.

**Deverbal nouns** (names of actions) form from any verb stem with -ma/-me or -uw/-üw/-w.

**Voices:** four — reflexive, passive, reciprocal, causative — against a basic fifth (active).

### Tenses (indicative)

1. **Present indefinite** — timeless or habitual action.
2. **Past categorical** — past facts, no distance or duration distinctions.
3. **Past subjective (non-evidential)** — hearsay: things the speaker knows from others, TV, radio; used in stories, tales, jokes.
4. **Present–future indefinite** — regular actions (*Ağam süt içmez* "My brother doesn't drink milk"); requests and offers (*Maña qalam berirsiñ mi?* "Will you give me a pencil?"); proverbs (*Ne ekseñ, şunu orursıñ* "You reap what you sow" — Նե եկսենկ, շունու օրուրսընկ); and future events that are not certain.
5. **Future categorical** — action definitely after the moment of speech.
6. **Present of the moment** — ongoing now; also future with a time word, or begun in the past and continuing.
7. **Past continuous** — stem + -a/-e/-y + auxiliary *edi* (եդի): kele edim, kele edik, kele ediñ, kele ediñiz, kele edi, kele ediler.
8. **Pluperfect** — -ğan/-gen/-qan/-ken or -mış/-miş/-muş/-müş + *edi*: alğan (almış) edim …

The person endings visible in the paradigms: 1sg -m, 1pl -k, 2sg -ñ, 2pl -ñiz, 3sg —, 3pl -ler.

### Converbs

Three forms: -ıp/-ip/-up/-üp/-p (barıp, kelip, söylep, körüp); -a/-e/-y with doubling (bara-bara, kele-kele); and -araq/-erek/-yaraq/-yerek. Like other verb forms, a converb relates its process to its doer implicitly, without special markers.

## Adjectives

Adjectives have no inflection of their own except degrees of comparison (positive, comparative, superlative) — their main difference from nouns. Used as subject or object, an adjective takes nominal inflection.

## Adverbs

Manner adverbs describe how an action is performed and answer "how?"
