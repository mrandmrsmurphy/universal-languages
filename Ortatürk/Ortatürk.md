Neutral means of communication across the Turkic world, from Turkey across Azerbaijan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, with Turkic regions in China and Siberia.

## Uniting features

- Vowel harmony
- Agglutinative structure
- Regularized morphology (fewer Arabic/Persian irregularities than Ottoman Turkish)
- Consistent SOV
- Averaged lexicon computed on the "double majority" principle (by total speakers and by number of languages)

## Lineage

- **Historical predecessor:** Ismail Gaspirali's 19th-century pan-Turkic project ("Jalpi Türk"), promoted through his journal *Terciman*.
- **1992:** Baxtiyar Kärimov and Shoahmad Mutalov devise the averaging formula, demonstrated on 5 Turkic languages (Azerbaijani, Kazakh, Tatar, Turkish, Uzbek).
- **2005:** second version adds Kyrgyz, Turkmen, Uyghur.
- **2008:** the World Assembly of Turkic Peoples (VATN) founds an institute for Ortatürk and builds a simplified version computed over 25 Turkic languages (~160 million speakers).
- **2020s:** VK community promotes the language as "Öztürkçe"; **2023:** V.A. Mireev publishes *Öztürk Tili* with grammar, a 16,000-word dictionary, and translations.

The 1992 proposal covered only Common Turkic (omitting the Oghuric, Siberian, and Argu/Khalaj branches); the 2008 VATN program's data includes the distant branches too — Chuvash, Sakha, Dolgan, Salar, Khalaj, the Siberian languages — plus historical languages (Old Turkic, Chagatai, Armeno-Kipchak, Kashgari's Middle Turkic, and others) at zero speaker weight.
