## Vowels

| | Front | Back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | i | u |
| Mid | e | o |
| Low | a | |

Five vowels, with no height contrast among the mid vowels and no length contrast.

**Stress:** penultimate.

**Tone:** high tone is written with an acute (á é í ó ú); low tone is unmarked (a e i o u). Tone is lexical and lives on roots: each root is specified high or low (a noun stem carries one high syllable at most), and all affixes — class prefixes, the negative *ka-*, subject and object markers, tense–aspect markers, extensions, final vowels — are toneless and surface low. Since every grammatical distinction in Kintu is affixal, tone never carries grammar, and the language stays simple for speakers of both tonal and non-tonal languages.

## Phonotactics

Syllables are (N)C(G)V: an optional homorganic nasal, one consonant, an optional glide (y/w), one vowel. There are no consonant clusters beyond prenasalization, and no final consonants. Vowel hiatus is resolved by glide insertion.

## From Proto-Bantu

Proto-Bantu had eleven consonants (*p t c k, b d j g, m n ɲ*), seven vowels — first-degree *i *u, second-degree (“super-close”) *ɪ *ʊ, and *e a o* — with a length contrast, and high/low tone. The changes from Proto-Bantu to Kintu apply in historical order:

1. **Partial spirantization before second-degree \*ɪ.** The voiceless stops \*p and \*k spirantize to *f* and *s* before the super-close front vowel \*ɪ — root-internally only. Voiced stops, \*t, prenasalized clusters, and grammatical prefixes are unaffected (compare Zulu *umuthi*, which likewise keeps \*t). This is the one native source of *f*, and a second source of *s*. The stronger spirantizations of the daughters (Swahili *-fupi*, Kinyarwanda *-gufi* < \*kʊ́pɪ́) did not generalize.
2. **Palatalization after first-degree \*i** (the “preceding-I effects” of the southern languages): a close front vowel palatalized a following consonant (*n → ɲ, s → ʃ-type outcomes). This supplies part of the ɲ and ʃ material.
3. **PB \*c → s** — the eastern reflex (Swahili, Luganda, Kinyarwanda).
4. **PB \*d → d / l:** *d* word-initially and after nasals, *l* between vowels.
5. **Vowel merger: \*ɪ → i, \*ʊ → u** (7 → 5) — the majority merger across the zone. It is ordered after the consonant changes so their close-vowel conditioning is still in force; the merger then erases the trigger.
6. **Vowel length dropped.**
7. **Loans** add *h*, *r*, *v* and *z*.

Throughout, the PB high/low tone contrast is preserved on roots; only high is written.
