# Anthropology

> Parent: [[Kintu]]

![[Bantu_flag.png\|180]]

Kintu is the Universal Language for the Bantusphere — the Bantu-speaking belt of central, eastern and southern Africa, about 450 million people. Its base is Proto-Bantu with modifications: the reconstruction is taken as already leaning in the directions the modern languages went, so that, for example, Swahili is treated as always having been popular. It is strictly Bantu; Khoisan, Cushitic, Nilotic and West African languages are outside the zone.

## Background

The Bantu languages descend from a single ancestor spoken in the Cameroon–Nigeria borderland a few thousand years ago. Their speakers spread south and east across the continent, and the languages remain unusually close: the same noun-class system, verb-prefix structure and much basic vocabulary run from Cameroon to the Cape. Roughly 500 languages are counted, with no sharp boundary between many of them.

Several Bantu languages already serve as regional lingua francas: Swahili across eastern Africa, Lingala in the Congo basin, Chichewa in Malawi and Zambia, and Zulu and Shona in their regions. Swahili is by far the largest as a second language, but it has not become a pan-Bantu bridge, and across the zone colonial languages (English, French, Portuguese) remain the default for cross-border communication.

## Languages

The zone follows Guthrie's regional groupings. A representative set is consulted from each, kept small enough to be workable.

- **East:** Swahili, Gikuyu, Kinyarwanda, Kirundi, Luganda
- **Central:** Lingala, Tshiluba, Kikongo, Bemba
- **Southern:** Shona, Nyanja (Chichewa), Zulu, Xhosa, Sotho, Tswana
- **Southwest:** Umbundu, Herero

The working check set — the columns of the Swadesh table and the languages every coinage is tested against — is six: **Swahili, Kinyarwanda, Lingala, Zulu, Shona, Umbundu**. They cover all four regions, include the family's largest second-language networks, and keep two seven-vowel languages (Lingala, Umbundu) in the set so the vowel-merger and spirantization decisions are always checked against daughters that never made them. Kinyarwanda stands in for Kirundi; the others in the full list remain background sources.

Swahili is far larger than any of its neighbours and would dominate any simple vote, so the border regions are kept well clear of its weight: the Bantu languages of the Somali coast and the Comoros, and the Bantu languages of Cameroon, Gabon and the West African borderlands, are not consulted.

## Weighting

Vocabulary and grammar start from the Proto-Bantu reconstruction, adjusted toward the forms the modern languages share. The daughter languages serve as a check on recognition rather than as a vote. The reconstruction matters, but not at the expense of the present: the Bantu homeland lies in the north, while the family's centre of gravity has moved steadily south, so the modern languages are weighted by where the speakers now are rather than by closeness to the homeland.
