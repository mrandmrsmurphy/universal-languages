# Design Philosophy

> Hub: [[Universal Languages]] · Scale: [[Development Roadmap]] · Compare: [[Language Comparisons]]

The principles that apply across all fifteen Universal Languages, each with its reason. Language-specific rationale lives on each language's own Anthropology and Orthography pages; Middle Semitic and Dan'a'yo keep their own Design Philosophy and README in their own repositories.

## One language per region, not one language for the world

Esperanto's scope was too broad: the sprinkles of German, Polish and Russian were too few and too unusual to make anyone happy. A language that unites a group related by descent or bound by a sprachbund can draw on a real shared stratum, so it feels familiar to its speakers while being far easier to learn than any natural language of the region.

## Neutral, not native

A UL is a hallway, not a home. No ethnic or national group owns it, and it replaces no one's mother tongue. Where a population is lopsided (Arabic against Hebrew, Russian against Sorbian), the design weighs source languages by number of languages and by neutrality, not by speaker count alone, so that the largest voice does not simply win.

## A shared stratum first

Each UL is built from the deepest layer its sources share:

- a **proto-language** (Proto-Germanic, Proto-Slavic, Proto-Bantu, Proto-Dravidian);
- a **classical language** (Sanskrit, Classical Chinese);
- or a **sprachbund consensus** where no common ancestor exists (MSEAL, SEDES, Balkan).

Only when that search is exhausted does a UL fall back to a modern majority among its sources.

## Easier than any other regional language

For every speaker in its zone, the UL must be easier to learn than any other regional language is for that speaker. This is the test applied to each contested choice: sounds, script, grammar and vocabulary.

## Its own script

Each UL has a unique writing system, so that it is never mistaken for a national language and so that its spelling can match its sounds. Interim Latin or source-script writing is acceptable while the real script is being fitted (see each language's Orthography page).

## Adopt what exists

Where a UL already exists as a well-developed project (Interlingua, Interslavic, Guosa, Ortatürk), the vault documents and adopts it instead of reforming it, and records only where its own choices differ.

## Boundaries follow linguistics, not politics

A zone includes the languages that fit its shared stratum and typology, even across borders. A language that would break too many universals of its zone moves to the group where it fits (Bengali moves from Neo-Sanskrit to MSEAL). Regions under about 50 million speakers are left out of the fifteen and may join a UL group by choice.

## Document the current state, keep the history separate

Content pages describe the language as it is now. What was decided when, and what was corrected, lives in `logs/log.md`. Claims whose evidence is weak are flagged on the page itself, tersely and without dates.

## Own the data

There is no external authority to defer to for most of these languages. Evidence is gathered per word and recorded with its strength, and a missing value means "not checked", never "absent".
