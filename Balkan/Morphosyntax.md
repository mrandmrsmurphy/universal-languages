# Morphosyntax

> Parent: [[Balkan]]

Balkan is SVO, fusional, and nominative–accusative. True to the sprachbund, grammatical relations lean on prepositions and word order; the case system is reduced.

## Nouns and adjectives

Nouns and adjectives inflect for three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter), three cases, and two numbers. Gender and case distinctions generally flatten in the plural.

The cases:

- **Nominative**
- **Accusative**
- **Oblique** — the merged genitive and dative (as in Greek, Albanian and Romanian), always governed by a preposition

The vocative is expressed with a dedicated preposition (compare Albanian *o*).

## Articles

- The **definite article** is postposed, sprachbund-style.
- The **indefinite article** is preposed, and is simply a form of *en* 'one'.

The polite second person is the second-person plural: to address one person politely, use βη and a plural verb. In writing, polite βη may take a capital (Βη). The familiar τη is for intimates, children and equals.

## Verbs

- **Future:** the particle *da*, reduced from the verb 'want' (compare Greek *θα*, Albanian *do*, Bulgarian *ще*), before the verb. There is no synthetic future.
- **Perfect:** the verb 'have' plus an invariable past participle.
- **Evidentiality:** a single marking on the verb for information that is reported, inferred or unexpected, as in Turkish -miş, the Albanian admirative and the Bulgarian renarrative. The unmarked form claims direct knowledge.
- **Infinitives:** replaced by subordinate clauses introduced by *da* (the same particle as the future: *da* directly before the verb in a main clause is future; after another verb or a conjunction it marks a subordinate clause).

*Verb forms (the 'want' and 'have' verbs, the participle, the evidential marker, the subordinate-clause marker) have not yet been coined.*
