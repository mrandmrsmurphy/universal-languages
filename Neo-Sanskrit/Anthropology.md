# Anthropology

> Parent: [[Neo-Sanskrit]]

**Neo-Sanskrit** is the Universal Language for the Indo-Aryan subcontinent, about 1.4 billion people. The foundation is Sanskrit, and the design is "clipped Sanskrit": native words as shortened Sanskrit forms, made regular.

## The zone

The branches covered: Central, Dardic, Northern, Northwestern, Southern, and Western Indo-Aryan. Eastern (Bengali and its sisters) is deliberately excluded — it would break too many universals of the remaining group — and goes with [[MSEAL]]. The Dravidian south has its own UL, [[Dravindian]], though Dravidian shaped the region's phonology.

## From Sanskrit to today

Many of the modern languages went through Prakritization, but not all did — so rather than imitate any one historical path, Neo-Sanskrit makes its own rules to get from Sanskrit to today:

- Weak final schwa deletion
- Cluster reduction and simplification
- Syllabic ṛ is kept; syllabic ḷ becomes li
