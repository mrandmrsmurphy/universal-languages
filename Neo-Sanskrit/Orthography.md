# Orthography

> Parent: [[Neo-Sanskrit]] · Sounds: [[Neo-Sanskrit/Phonology]]

The eventual script is **Kaithi**, the historical mercantile script of the eastern Gangetic plain. Romanization follows IAST habits (ṭ ḍ ṇ ś ṣ; aspirated stops written ph, th, ṭh, ch, kh; breathy bh, dh, ḍh, jh, gh).

- The breathy series keeps its own letters even though no minimal pairs oppose it to the plain voiced series (see [[Neo-Sanskrit/Phonology]]).
- ṇa, ña, and ŋa are one phoneme; the three letters are positional variants and interchangeable.
- va and ha are marginal phonemes.

## Vowels

Independent letters: 𑂃 a, 𑂄 ā, 𑂅 i, 𑂆 ī, 𑂇 u, 𑂈 ū, 𑂉 e, 𑂊 ai, 𑂋 o, 𑂌 au. The diacritic forms are in the chart below.

### Syllabic ṛ

Kaithi never had a letter for vocalic ṛ, so one is borrowed: the convention comes from **Tirhuta** (Kaithi's sister script of Mithila, which has a true vocalic-ṛ sign), rendered with **U+0327 combining cedilla**, since the Tirhuta codepoints themselves don't render on common machines.

- After a consonant, the cedilla replaces the inherent a: kṛta = 𑂍̧𑂞, tṛṣṇā = 𑂞̧𑂭𑂝𑂰, smṛti = 𑂮𑂹𑂧̧𑂞𑂱
- Word-initial (independent): ra with cedilla — ṛṣi = 𑂩̧𑂭𑂱

Romanization stays IAST ṛ.
