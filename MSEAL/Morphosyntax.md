# Morphosyntax

> Parent: [[MSEAL]]

MSEAL is isolating and analytic: words are mostly monomorphemic, with almost no inflection and little affixation. Grammatical relations are signaled by word order, particles, and coverbs or prepositions; modality is expressed with sentence-final particles.

## Word formation

1. Cə- prefixes → abstract or grammatical meaning
2. Compounding → new concepts (e.g., "fire mountain" for volcano)
3. Reduplication → plurality, emphasis, or aspect
4. Serial verbs → complex actions (e.g., "go buy eat" = go buy food)

The minor syllable of a sesquisyllable is always Cə- (see [[MSEAL/Phonology]]).

## Syntax

- SVO word order, with heavy postnominal modification: relative clauses follow the noun.
- Topic–comment structure: information flow prioritizes topics over strict subject–predicate structure.
- Serial verb constructions: verbs chain without overt conjunctions ("go buy eat" rather than "go to buy and eat").
- Aspect, not tense: post-verbal aspectual markers and sentence-final particles; there is no tense inflection.
- Sentence-final particles also carry modality, politeness, evidentiality, and discourse functions (compare Mandarin 吗 ma, Thai นะ ná).
- Negation uses a dedicated pre-verbal particle (compare Mandarin 不 bù, Vietnamese không).
- Directional verbs: motion verbs specify direction explicitly ("go up," "go down," "come in").
- Reduplication marks pluralization, intensity, or aspectual modification (compare Thai ดีๆ di di "very good").

## Classifiers

Classifiers are required when counting nouns. MSEAL has five sortal classifiers: **human**, **animal**, **plant**, **object** (the default for inanimates, absorbing the long/flat/round shape distinctions the zone languages cut differently), and **abstract** (matters, events, kinds). Measure and group words (cup of, pair of, flock of) are an open class, as in every language of the zone.
