# Phonology

> Parent: [[MSEAL]]

MSEAL has monosyllabic morphemes, lexical tone, a fairly large inventory of consonants including phonemic aspiration, no initial clusters, and plentiful vowel contrasts. Words may be sesquisyllabic (see below).

## Vowels

| | Front | Central | Back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | i | ə | u |
| Mid | e | | o |
| Low | a | | |

/ə/ ranges from high to mid. Diphthongs: ai, au, ei, ou.

## Codas

Syllables may end in one of seven consonants: p, t, k, m, n, ŋ, ʔ.

## Tones

Four tones: level, rising, departing, and checked. Open and nasal-final syllables are *live* and carry level, rising, or departing (level is unmarked). The checked tone is confined to stop-final syllables (-p, -t, -k, -ʔ), as in every tonal language of the zone (Cantonese, Vietnamese, Thai, Burmese).

## Syllables and sesquisyllables

Syllables are CV or CVC; there are no consonant clusters. Words are at most sesquisyllabic: a minor syllable of the shape Cə- precedes the stressed main syllable, giving Cə-CV or Cə-CVC. The minor syllable bears no tone, and fricatives do not occur as its onset.
