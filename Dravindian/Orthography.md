# Orthography

> Parent: [[Dravindian]]

Dravindian is written in the Brāhmī script. The language's name: 𑀩𑁆𑀭𑀸𑀳𑁆𑀫𑀻 (bra.mi).

## Vowels

| | Front short | Front long | Back short | Back long |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | /i/ 𑀇 | /iː/ 𑀈 | /u/ 𑀉 | /uː/ 𑀊 |
| Mid | /e/ 𑁱 | /eː/ 𑀏 | /o/ 𑁲 | /oː/ 𑀑 |
| Low | | | /a/ 𑀅 | /aː/ 𑀆 |

Diphthongs: /ai/ 𑀐 and /au/ 𑀒 (signs 𑁃 and 𑁅 after a consonant).

Brāhmī 𑀏 and 𑀑 are the long vowels /eː/ and /oː/, as in Tamil-Brāhmī. The short vowels /e/ and /o/ use the Old Tamil letters 𑁱 and 𑁲 (signs 𑁳 and 𑁴 after a consonant).

## Vowel signs

After a consonant the inherent vowel is /a/; the other vowels are written with signs, and 𑁆 (virama) cancels the inherent vowel.

| /a/ | /aː/ | /i/ | /iː/ | /u/ | /uː/ | /e/ | /eː/ | /o/ | /oː/ | virama |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (inherent) | 𑀸 | 𑀺 | 𑀻 | 𑀼 | 𑀽 | 𑁳 | 𑁂 | 𑁴 | 𑁄 | 𑁆 |

## Nasals

The velar, palatal and retroflex nasals (/ŋ/ 𑀗, /ɲ/ 𑀜, /ɳ/ 𑀡) each keep their own letter. The distinction is for spelling and roots only: no two words may differ in speech by these nasals alone.

## Labial fricative

/f/, /v/ and /ʋ/ are one phoneme, written 𑀯 in every case.

## Spacing and punctuation

Words are separated by spaces. The danda 𑁇 ends a sentence.

## Romanization

Used only where the script cannot be shown. It follows ISO 15919 / IAST habits:

| --- | --- |
