# Anthropology

> Parent: [[Dravindian]]

![[Drivindian_flag.svg\|180]]

Dravindian is a Universal Language for the Dravidian languages of southeast India. Its sources are Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Tulu, Gondi, and reconstruction from Proto-Dravidian. It is the sister project to [[Neo-Sanskrit]], covering the subcontinent's other major family.

## Family features

The design rests on the family's shared traits:

- **Eight cases**
- **Three genders**
- **Clusivity** — inclusive vs. exclusive 'we'
- **Agglutinative morphology**
- **Five vowels**, short and long
- **Gemination** of consonants
- **T–V distinction** in the second person
- **Negation marked on verbs**
- **Polar questions marked on the verb**
- **SOV** word order
- **Stops contrast voiced and unvoiced only** in native words — no aspirated or creaky series

## Why separate from Neo-Sanskrit

The two families share a subcontinent and a long contact history, which is exactly why they could be mistaken for one zone. They are kept apart because the shared surface hides different grammars:

- **Unrelated families.** Dravidian and Indo-Aryan have no common ancestor; core vocabulary does not match, and Sanskrit words in Dravidian are loans.
- **Shared retroflexes are areal, not inherited.** Indo-Aryan acquired its retroflex series through contact with Dravidian, so the common dental~retroflex grid does not mean a common phonology.
- **Aspiration and breathy voice.** Native Dravidian has neither; Neo-Sanskrit keeps the full Sanskrit 4×5 stop grid. Here the aspirated and breathy series exist only for loanwords.
- **Alignment.** Neo-Sanskrit has split ergativity; Dravidian is plainly nominative–accusative.
- **Morphology.** Dravidian is strictly agglutinative and suffixing, with verbal negation and a verbal polar-question marker; Neo-Sanskrit is built on clipped Sanskrit with postpositions and formal (shape-based) gender.
- **Person system.** Dravidian has clusivity (inclusive vs. exclusive 'we') and a T–V distinction; Indo-Aryan largely lacks clusivity.
- **Syllable shape.** Dravidian words are CVN at most, with gemination and no initial clusters; Neo-Sanskrit permits CCVCC.
- **Vowels.** Dravidian has five vowels with short/long contrast and no syllabic ṛ.
- **Universals test.** Merging the families would break too many universals of each, the same reasoning that excludes Bengali from Neo-Sanskrit.

## Source languages

Zone: South India and Sri Lanka, with the rest of the family consulted for reconstruction. Languages are ranked by weight in the design: population, branch coverage, and how much older structure they preserve. Speaker figures are approximate (2011 Census of India mother-tongue counts, rounded).

| Tier | Language | Branch | Speakers | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Telugu | South-Central | ~81 M | Largest; South-Central anchor |
| 1 | Tamil | South | ~69 M | Most conservative literary language; Sri Lanka; the main source for Proto-Dravidian comparison |
| 1 | Kannada | South | ~44 M | Third largest; best WALS coverage |
| 1 | Malayalam | South | ~35 M | Close to Tamil; separate national standard |
| 2 | Gondi | South-Central | ~3 M (higher in some estimates) | Largest tribal language; central India; non-literary check |
| 2 | Tulu | South | ~2 M | Coastal Karnataka; seven or more vowels; check on southern vowel system |
| 3 | Kurukh (Kurux) | North | ~2 M | Only northern-branch check in India; tests which features are family-wide |
| 3 | Brahui | North | ~1–4 M | Pakistan, far outside the zone; consulted only for reconstruction |
| 3 | Kui, Kuvi, Konda, Kolami, Koya, Kodava, Toda, Kota | various | small | Reconstruction evidence only |
| Reference | Proto-Dravidian | — | — | Foundation; Tamil and Kurukh/Brahui are the key witnesses |

The four literary languages are the voting core; the tier-2 and tier-3 languages check recognizability and structure rather than vote. Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Tulu and Gondi are the named sources; Kurukh and Brahui are added as the northern-branch witnesses.
