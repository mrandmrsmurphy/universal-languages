# Morphosyntax

> Parent: [[Dravindian]]

Dravindian is SOV, agglutinative, suffixing only, and nominative–accusative. Heads follow their dependents: adjectives and genitives precede the noun, and case and other relations are marked by suffixes or postpositions.

## Nouns

- **Cases:** eight — nominative, accusative, dative, genitive, locative, ablative, instrumental, sociative. The vocative is the bare stem. Each case has one regular ending, with no oblique stems and no sandhi.
- **Gender:** three — rational masculine, rational feminine, and neuter. Gender is semantic, not formal: it shows only in third-person pronouns and verb agreement, never on noun stems.
- **Number:** singular and plural; the plural suffix is -kaḷ 𑀓𑀴𑁆. Number is optional after a numeral.
- **Objects:** the accusative marks animate or definite objects; other objects may be unmarked.
- **Experiencers:** subjects of feeling, liking, wanting and possession take the dative.

## Pronouns

Clusivity: 'we' is inclusive (speaker and addressee) or exclusive (speaker and others). The polite second person is the plural.

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (plural exclusive) | nān 𑀦𑀸𑀦𑁆 | nāṅ 𑀦𑀸𑀗𑁆 |
| 1 inclusive | — | nām 𑀦𑀸𑀫𑁆 |
| 2 familiar | nī 𑀦𑀻 | nīm 𑀦𑀻𑀫𑁆 |
| 2 polite | nīm 𑀦𑀻𑀫𑁆 | nīm 𑀦𑀻𑀫𑁆 |

Third-person pronouns are built from the demonstrative bases i- (this), a- (that) and e- (which?), with gender and number endings:

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| this, sg. | ivan 𑀇𑀯𑀦𑁆 | ivaḷ 𑀇𑀯𑀴𑁆 | idu 𑀇𑀤𑀼 |
| that, sg. | avan 𑀅𑀯𑀦𑁆 | avaḷ 𑀅𑀯𑀴𑁆 | adu 𑀅𑀤𑀼 |
| this, pl. | ivar 𑀇𑀯𑀭𑁆 (rational) | | ivu 𑀇𑀯𑀼 |
| that, pl. | avar 𑀅𑀯𑀭𑁆 (rational) | | avu 𑀅𑀯𑀼 |

The rational plural avar also serves as the honorific singular.

## Verbs

- **Tense:** past, non-past and future suffixes.
- **Agreement:** person and number; gender only in the third person.
- **Negation:** a verb suffix that replaces the tense marker; a negative existential verb.
- **Polar questions:** a verb-final suffix.
- **Copula:** none; predicate nominals take person suffixes. Existence and possession use a separate existential verb.
- **Non-finite forms:** a converb ('having done'), a relative participle placed before the noun, an infinitive, a verbal noun, and a conditional.
- **Chaining:** clauses are joined by converbs, with the finite verb last; the clitic -um joins nouns ('also', 'and').
- **Quotative:** the verb 'say' marks quoted speech and complement clauses.
- **Auxiliaries:** aspect and benefaction by auxiliary verbs after the main verb; causatives are productive.

## Other

- **Questions:** wh-words stay in place (not fronted).
- **Echo words:** reduplication with a changed first syllable gives 'and the like'.
- **Numerals:** decimal, no classifiers.

*Forms for case endings, verb paradigms and the demonstrative-derived interrogatives have not yet been coined. The pronoun stems follow the Proto-Dravidian reconstructions, simplified; the first-person plural pair nām/nāṅ is an adaptation of the Tamil–Malayalam pattern and is not directly attested in all sources.*
