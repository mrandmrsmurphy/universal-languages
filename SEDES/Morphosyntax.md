# Morphosyntax

> Parent: [[SEDES]]

The design aims at a coherent, readily parsed system rather than a statistically average one.

## Clause

| Feature | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Basic clause order | Subject–object–verb |
| Adpositions | Postpositions; the case endings are postpositions too |
| Possessor | Before the possessed noun |
| Relative clause | Before the noun, with an explicit boundary marker |
| Adjectives | After the noun, to be tested against alternatives |
| Questions | One invariant clause-final question particle |
| Negation | One invariant preverbal negative particle |

## Nouns

- Two genders.
- Case is marked by postpositions, which are suffixed and obligatory: the nominative is *-ni* and the accusative is *-na*. Pronouns may omit the case ending when the role is obvious. The alignment is marked nominative, a kind of nominative–accusative alignment. The case suffixes also mark definiteness and focus.
- Possessive pronominals are most likely suffixes on the noun, as in Semitic; free-standing possessive pronouns are the alternative, and the choice is not yet decided.
- One productive plural suffix. Number marking is optional after an explicit numeral.
- No noun-class agreement.
- An inclusive–exclusive "we" is postponed until testing shows a real benefit.

## Verbs

- One invariant lexical stem.
- Tense, aspect and mood (TAM) are marked after the verb, optionally: a clause may end in a TAM marker or go without one (past, present, future and others). Whether the marker is a bound suffix or an attached particle is not yet decided, and the difference is small.
- No obligatory person or number agreement; with a marked nominative, agreement would be double marking.
- Regular causative, passive and reciprocal derivation.
- One productive verbal-noun ending and one productive converb ending.
- Semitic-derived words are borrowed as whole stems; there is no productive consonantal root-and-pattern morphology. A borrowed verb may keep its inherited internal pattern, but its new derivatives follow the regular rules.

### Converbs

A converb is a dependent verb form used for sequencing actions or expressing accompanying circumstances, and its use is among the strongest recurring grammatical connections across the region:

> market-to go-CONVERB, grain buy-PAST  
> "Having gone to the market, [someone] bought grain."

The construction is explicit. There is one invariant converb ending. The subject is the same as that of the following clause by default, and a new overt subject is used when the subject changes. Separate particles express "before", "because" and "although", so the converb does not have to carry all of them.

### Light verbs

A content word combined with a very general verb such as "say" or "do" forms a complex predicate, with an invariant coverb and an inflecting light verb. The construction is productive and is an easy way to extend the lexicon without a new root for every activity.
