The ejectives are contrastive phonemes, not optional pronunciations of the plain stops. All four occur in Oromo, and ejective series are also characteristic of Amharic and Tigrinya, so keeping them preserves distinctions in borrowed vocabulary and aids recognition.

## Vowels

SEDES has five vowel qualities, /i e a o u/, each short or long, written i ii · e ee · a aa · o oo · u uu. There is no central vowel and never an ä; an Amharic ä in a loanword is adapted to a or e word by word, and recurring correspondences are then regularized. No grammatical distinction rests on vowel length alone.

## Prosody

There is no stress, tone or pitch accent. Consonants may be geminated.

## Syllables

Syllables are CV or CVC, so every syllable has an onset: a word that begins with a vowel begins with a glottal stop, /ʔ/ (written with the አ series in Geʿez). The glottals (/ʔ/, /h/) and the ejectives (/pʼ tʼ čʼ kʼ/) do not occur in the coda, and the glottals and ejectives are never geminated.
