# Phonology

> Parent: [[Interslavic]]

## Vowels

| | Front | Back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | /i/ и | /u/ у |
| Near-high | /ɪ/ ы | |
| Mid | /ɛ/ е, /jɛ/ є | /ɔ/ о |
| Low | /a/ а (central) | |

**Interslavic** has the five cardinal vowels (a e i o u) and the near-high front vowel /ɪ/. The mid vowels are transcribed loosely: /ɛ/ and /ɔ/ may equally be written /e/ and /o/. When harmonizing words across languages, a sound that one language has and another lacks is mapped to the nearest Interslavic vowel: /ɑ/ is treated as /a/, and /ə/ as /ɛ/. There is also syllabic /r/, which may be preceded by a schwa as needed, with no difference in meaning.

The tilde marks accepted variants: the inventory accommodates speakers from diverse backgrounds within the Slavic world, so a range of pronunciations is acceptable, including retroflex ones. There is only one /r/. There are no other palatalized consonants.

## Phonotactics

- Hard: the labials p b f v m, the alveolars t d s z n r l and the velars k g h
- Soft: the postalveolars š ž č dž, the affricates c dz, and j

/ɪ/ can only occur after a hard consonant, but never after a velar.

### Palatalization

Palatalization means that under certain circumstances the velar consonants k g h are changed to the postalveolar consonants č ž š. This happens when they are followed by an ending with -e-, -i- or -j-, as well as before the suffixes -ny, -ski, -nik, -stvo, -ok, -ka, -ko. For example: mog- + -eš > možeš, ruk- + -ny > ručny, muh- + ka > muška.

### Softening

Softening is the process of adding a [ʲ] to a consonant, resulting in a more palatal pronunciation. The number of soft consonants in the phoneme inventory varies greatly from one language to another. In Interslavic only two soft consonants are obligatory: ʎ and ñ.

### Iotation

- The combinations s+j and z+j become š ž.
- The velars k g h, when followed by j, palatalize into č ž š.
- In the sequences sk st zg zd, the s and z are iotated along with the following consonant.
- š ž č j simply swallow the following j.
