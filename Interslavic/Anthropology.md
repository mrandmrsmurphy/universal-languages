# Anthropology

> Parent: [[Interslavic]]

**Interslavic** (Medžuslovjansky, Меджусловйанскы) is the pan-Slavic Universal Language. Its purpose is to facilitate communication between speakers of various Slavic languages, and to let people who do not speak a Slavic language communicate with Slavic speakers by being mutually intelligible with most, if not all, Slavic languages. Its use spans a broad range of fields, including tourism and education.

It is essentially a modern continuation of Old Church Slavonic, and it also draws on the macaronic languages Slavs have used for centuries to communicate between nationalities, for example in multi-Slavic environments and on the Internet, giving them a scientific base. Both grammar and vocabulary are therefore based on elements common to the Slavic languages. Its main focus is instant intelligibility rather than easy learning, a balance typical of naturalistic (as opposed to schematic) languages.

Design goals, in descending order of importance:

1. instant intelligibility to speakers of any Slavic language
2. ease of learning

## History

The Interslavic project began in 2006 under the name Slovianski. In 2011 Slovianski underwent a thorough reform and merged with two other projects (both now defunct); the result was called Interslavic, a name first proposed by the Czech Ignác Hošek in 1908. Two applications for an ISO 639-3 code, in 2012 and 2014, failed. A third request was filed in September 2019, and the code `isv` was adopted in April 2024.

## Adoption

Interslavic occupies a unique sociopolitical niche as a constructed pan-Slavic auxiliary language designed to bridge communication among the diverse Slavic peoples of Central and Eastern Europe. It is mutually intelligible with speakers of Russian, Polish, Czech, Serbo-Croatian, Bulgarian, and others, but it faces an uphill battle for adoption.

The major challenge lies in the deeply entrenched national identities tied to language across the Slavic world. For most Slavic nations, language is not merely a tool of communication but a symbol of cultural sovereignty and historical struggle, often against other Slavic powers. As such, proposals for a neutral, supranational Slavic tongue are sometimes perceived as threatening or naive, even though Interslavic makes no attempt to supplant native tongues.

The political fragmentation of the Slavic world, spanning EU and non-EU states, NATO members and Russian allies, complicates efforts to rally institutional support behind a single unifying auxiliary. Interslavic thrives in online communities, YouTube channels, and grassroots cultural projects, but its growth is largely bottom-up, driven by hobbyists, linguists, and idealists rather than governments or education ministries. Until a shared regional need, perhaps in trade, ecological cooperation, or pan-Slavic media, emerges to justify a common working language, adoption is constrained by realpolitik and national loyalties.

## Script

Interslavic is written in Latin or Cyrillic letters. Latin is needed for the time being so that Czechs, Poles and other Latin-script Slavs can read it; the long-term goal is Cyrillic. See [[Interslavic/Orthography]].

## Background

More than almost any other Universal Language, the Slavs have a pre-existing unity: roughly 1,000 years ago they shared a common language, known as [Common Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Slavic) and later [Old Church Slavonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic). The Slavic languages have since fractured into three families: Eastern, Western, and Southern.

## Voting procedure

The contributions of the Eastern languages count as one vote in vocabulary, since they are almost mutually intelligible and they have won the orthography battle.

- **Czech and Slovak** are broadly similar.
- **Polish (and Silesian)** have diverged quite radically. No other Slavic language has retroflex consonants, so those are not part of the inventory, though they are accepted as variants (see [[Interslavic/Phonology]]).
- **Sorbian** is not really unified: Upper and Lower Sorbian are exceedingly different from each other.
- **Southern Slavic** may be consulted but does not vote, because it votes in [[Balkan]]. It remains part of the audience.

## Languages consulted

[Proto-Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Slavic) and the following are consulted at every stage:

1. Czech, Slovak
2. Polish (and Silesian)
3. Sorbian
4. Russian, Belarusian, Ukrainian, Rusyn
