# Orthography

> Parent: [[Interslavic]]

The long-term script is Cyrillic. Latin is used in the interim so that readers of Czech, Polish and other Latin-script Slavic languages can read Interslavic; the lexicon gives a Latin romanization for each word. The letter j is written й in Cyrillic. Etymological Latin diacritics resolve to plain letters: ě is e (Cyrillic е), ȯ is o (о), ų is u (у), ę is e (е), and ť ď ň ř ľ ś are t d n r l s. Only Є (je) marks a j before e.

Six vowels = the Big 5 + /ɪ~ɨ/, p/t/k, b/d/g, f/s/š, h~x, v/z/ž, c/č, dz/dž, m/n/ñ, r, l~ʎ, j

## Russian comparison

- **Other:** ~~Ъъ~~, ~~Ьь~~
- **Ligatures:** Єє = je, Юю = ju, Яя = ja
- **Letters not in Russian:** Њњ (=ñ, /ɲ/), Љљ (=/ʎ/), Єє
- **Letter sequences:** ДЗ/дз (=dz) and ДЖ/дж (=dž) are written with two letters, not single digraph letters. Ж/ж is ž.
