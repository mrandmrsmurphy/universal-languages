## Alignment and case

- **Ergative alignment, consistently**: S=P (absolutive) vs. A (ergative) marking holds across all tenses — no split.
- Cases beyond absolutive and ergative: **genitive, dative, locative, allative, ablative, perlative, instrumental, comitative** — ten cases total.

## Syntax

- SOV with some flexibility in word order.
- Relative clauses precede their head noun.

## Nominal and verbal categories

- No gender. No evidentiality. No clusivity.
- T–V distinction present.
- Polypersonal agreement on the verb; clear, agglutinative verbal morphology.

## Phonology

Six vowels: /i e a ə o u/, plus gemination. Stress is weight-based and predictable.

A large consonant inventory with the region's hallmark ejectives, though phonotactics don't get near the Georgian level of clustering:

## Orthography

Caucas is written in the Caucasian Albanian alphabet (left-to-right) — fitting, since the script was built for a language with the same ejective-heavy East Caucasian typology. Most of the inventory has native letters; the gaps are filled by repurposing letters for sounds Caucas doesn't have.

Two repurposed letters: 𐕞 (historical ü [y]) = /u/ — chosen over the script's historical o+w digraph (𐕒𐕡, the Armenian ու pattern) to keep one phoneme per letter — and 𐕈 (å [ɒ]) = /ə/.

Gemination is written by doubling the letter.

### Loan reserve

The unused historical letters are kept for etymological spelling of foreign words: 𐕆 /h/ (Russian, Persian loans), 𐔶 ē [ey], the voiced affricates 𐕑 /dʒ/ and 𐕕 /dz/, 𐕉 /tɕ/, 𐔹 /tɕʼ/, and the palatalized series 𐔿 𐕁 𐕅 𐕋 𐕏 𐕟 𐕓 (/nʲ dʲ lʲ tsʲ dzʲ tsʲʼ tʲʼ/).

## Sources

For an overview of the region's languages, see [Languages of the Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Caucasus).

The source list draws from both the indigenous Caucasian families (which define the areal typology) and the major contact families (significant regional populations and historical lingua francas).

**Indigenous families**

- **Kartvelian**: Georgian (primary reference — main regional literary and state language), Mingrelian, Laz, Svan
- **Northwest Caucasian**: Kabardian (East Circassian), Adyghe (West Circassian), Abkhaz, Abaza; historical Ubykh
- **Northeast Caucasian**: Nakh (Chechen, Ingush, Batsbi); Avaro-Andi-Tsezic (Avar — major inter-ethnic lingua franca in Dagestan — Andi, Tsez, Karata, Akhvakh); Lezgic (Lezgi, Tabasaran, Aghul, Rutul, Tsakhur, Udi); Lak-Dargwa (Dargwa, Lak); the isolate Khinalug

**Contact families**

- **Turkic**: Azerbaijani (major South Caucasus language and historical trade lingua franca), Karachay-Balkar, Kumyk (historical lingua franca of northern Dagestan), Nogai. These are zone members of [[Ortatürk]] (Azerbaijani was one of its five original averaging languages); here they are areal contributors only — shared, the way Romanian is shared between [[Interlingua]] and [[Balkan]].
- **Indo-European**: Armenian; Iranian (Ossetian Iron/Digor, Tat/Judeo-Tat, Talysh, Kurmanji Kurdish) — zone members of [[Zens]], the pan-Iranian UL, and areal contributors here only; Russian (dominant regional lingua franca across the entire Caucasus)

## Weighting

A three-tier weighting keeps the language structurally Caucasian while lexically recognizable across the region:

| Tier | Languages | Primary contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Major anchors | Georgian, Chechen, Avar, Azerbaijani, Armenian, Russian | Broad lexical roots, regional internationalisms, macro-grammar syntax |
| 2 — Sub-regional standards | Kabardian/Adyghe, Abkhaz, Lezgi, Ossetian, Kumyk, Karachay-Balkar | Phonological constraints, essential local vocabulary, case/aspect nuances |
| 3 — Minority/local reference | Mingrelian, Svan, Ingush, Lak, Dargwa, Tabasaran, Tat | Verification of pan-Caucasian areal traits (ejectives, schwa usage) |

The exhaustive source list may still be pruned; the languages outside all three tiers (Laz, Batsbi, the smaller Andi/Tsezic and Lezgic lects, Khinalug, Nogai, Talysh, Kurdish, Judeo-Tat, Ubykh) are the natural candidates.
