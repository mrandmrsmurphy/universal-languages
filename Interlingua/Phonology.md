# Phonology

> Parent: [[Interlingua]] · Script: [[Interlingua/Orthography]]

Interlingua is primarily a written language, and its pronunciation is not entirely settled. As a rule of thumb, consonants are pronounced as in English and vowels as in Spanish.

## Vowels

Five vowels, as in Spanish:

| | Front | Back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Close | i | u |
| Close-mid | e | o |
| Open | a | |

Five falling diphthongs: /ai/, /au/, /ei/, /eu/, /oi/ — /ei/ and /oi/ are rare.

## Letter-to-sound rules

The spelling is largely phonemic (26 letters, no diacritics — see [[Interlingua/Orthography]]). The non-obvious values:

- **c** = /ts/ (optionally /s/) before e, i, y; /k/ elsewhere
- **ch** = /ʃ/ in words of French origin (chef /ʃef/), /k/ in words of Greek and Italian origin (choro /koro/), /tʃ/ in words of English or Spanish origin (Chile); some words allow either (chocolate /tʃokolate/ or /ʃokolate/)
- **sc** before e, i, y has no settled value; /sts/ is common (scientia)
- **g** = /g/ always, even before e, i, y — unlike any Romance language; but final **-age** and some words of French origin take /(d)ʒ/ (orange, mangiar)
- **h** = /h/ or silent
- **q** appears only in **qu** = /kw/, except in que, qui and their derivatives (anque, proque), where it is /k/
- **s** between vowels is often /z/, but irregularly
- **t** + i + vowel is pronounced like c (/tsj/ or /sj/) unless the i is stressed or the t follows s
- **j** = /ʒ/, **w** = /w~v/, **x** = /ks/, **y** = /i/, **z** = /z/

Written double consonants may be geminated as in Italian, or pronounced single as in English or French.

## Stress

General rule: stress falls on the vowel before the last consonant — l**i**ngua, ess**e**r, requirim**e**nto — ignoring a final plural -(e)s (l**i**nguas). Where that is impossible, stress the first vowel: v**i**a, **i**o cr**e**a.

Exceptions:

- Nouns and adjectives ending in a vowel plus -le, -ne, or -re stress the third-from-last syllable: fr**a**gile, m**a**rgine, **a**ltere (but verbs: illa imp**o**ne)
- Endings -ica/-ico, -ide/-ido, -ula/-ulo also stress the third-from-last: pol**i**tica, scient**i**fico, r**a**pide, st**u**pido, cap**i**tula, s**e**culo
- Words in -ic stress the second-from-last: c**u**bic

Speakers may always fall back on the general rule: kilom**e**tro is acceptable beside the commoner kil**o**metro.

## Phonotactics and loanwords

Phonotactics are not explicitly defined; the prototyping procedure that selects Interlingua words favors forms most learners can pronounce (e.g. plural -es after a consonant, avoiding impossible clusters). Unassimilated loanwords keep their original spelling; diacritics are dropped when they don't affect pronunciation.
