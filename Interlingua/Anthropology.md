# Anthropology

> Parent: [[Interlingua]]

**Interlingua** is the Universal Language for the Romance world — French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Catalan, and their sisters. Unlike the other ULs it was not designed within this project: it is the finished 1951 product of the International Auxiliary Language Association (IALA), adopted here as-is. Its abbreviation is IA (not IL, which belongs to Peano's earlier Latino sine flexione, a close relative in both grammar and vocabulary). Romanian is shared with [[Balkan]] by design; English shaped the language as a control language but is not part of the zone it serves.

## History

IALA was founded in New York in 1924 by Alice Vanderbilt Morris and her husband Dave Hennen Morris, to put the auxiliary-language question on a scientific footing. Morris developed its research programme in consultation with Edward Sapir, William Edward Collinson, and Otto Jespersen, and IALA became a major patron of American linguistics, funding work by Sapir, Collinson, and Morris Swadesh among others. Its first decade went to surveying existing auxiliary languages — Esperanto, Ido, Novial, Occidental, Peano's Interlingua — with no intention of building a new one. By 1937 the membership had concluded that none was adequate, and a committee of 24 linguists from 19 universities published criteria for a new language before the war interrupted the work.

During the war years the naturalistic camp won the internal debate: thousands of usable international words already exist, and forcing learners to unlearn natural vocabulary in favor of a schematic derivation system was judged a Procrustean bed. E. Clark Stillman and Alexander Gode built the control-language methodology; when Stillman left for war work in 1943, Gode became Acting Director of Research. From 1946 to 1948, under André Martinet, IALA polled over 3,000 language professionals on four candidate models — P (highly naturalistic, Latin-like), M (moderately naturalistic), C (slightly schematic, Occidental-like), and K (moderately schematic, Ido-like). C and K were rejected (20% and 15%); M outpolled P (37.5% to 26.6%), and the final language was set as a compromise between M and P with repairs from C. Gode carried out the last phase after Martinet's 1948 resignation. Alice Morris died in 1950 and IALA's funding ended, but enough remained to publish the **Interlingua Grammar** and the **Interlingua–English Dictionary** in 1951, with *Interlingua a Prime Vista* following in 1954.

Interlingua's first decades were scientific: the newsletter *Spectroscopia Molecular* (1952–1980), bilingual summaries at medical congresses from 1954, and article summaries in some thirty scientific and medical journals into the late 1970s. IALA closed in 1953; promotion passed to Science Service, then to the Interlingua Institute (1970) and its successor the American Interlingua Society (2001), with the Union Mundial pro Interlingua (UMI) coordinating internationally — biennial conferences, a publishing programme of over a hundred volumes, and the bimonthly magazine *Panorama in Interlingua*.

Today the language competes with English as a lingua franca, but the internet has given it a second life: Interlingua editions of Wikipedia and Wiktionary exist, and it is occasionally taught at schools and universities as an on-ramp to the Romance languages and the international scientific vocabulary. Active speaker numbers are unknown and certainly small (the 2001 Hungarian census famously found two); its claim is not speakers but **comprehension** — it is the most widely understood auxiliary language, readable at sight by anyone familiar with a Romance language.

## Design

Interlingua is a naturalistic language built by extraction rather than reconstruction. Its vocabulary is not derived from Proto-Romance; it is the international vocabulary that already exists across the **control languages** — primary: English, French, Italian, Spanish/Portuguese; secondary: German, Russian.

- **Eligibility:** a word enters the vocabulary if attested in at least three of the four primary control languages (a secondary language may substitute for a primary). "Potential presence" counts: English *proximity* supports Interlingua *proxime*, since the Romance languages have the base word; Italian *fiammifero* 'flame-bearer' (a match) supports *flammifero* where other languages use a single morpheme.
- **Semantic discipline:** cognates whose meanings have drifted apart are treated as different words; a word enters only with the meanings still shared.
- **Fallback:** where the procedure yielded nothing (a few grammatical particles), Latin supplied the form.

Grammar follows the same minimality principle: **omit any feature absent from even one primary control language.** English eliminates noun gender, case, and agreement; French eliminates the progressive tenses; universal features like plural number and articles stay. The result is the smallest grammar the international vocabulary can carry — which is why the language has no grammatical gender despite every Romance source having it (and despite the sources disagreeing with each other about which nouns have which).
