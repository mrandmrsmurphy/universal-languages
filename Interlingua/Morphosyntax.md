# Morphosyntax

> Parent: [[Interlingua]] · Sounds: [[Interlingua/Phonology]] · Script: [[Interlingua/Orthography]]

The grammar is basically Romance, simplified under English influence, with German and Russian consulted. Any feature absent from even one primary control language was dropped: no adjectival agreement (absent in English), no continuous tenses (absent in French). Articles exist because Russian is only a secondary control. This page follows Gode & Blair (1951), which is accepted today but regarded as conservative.

There is no systemic marking for parts of speech. Typically: adjectives end in -e or a consonant, adverbs in -mente or -o, nouns in -a, -e, -o or a consonant; finite verbs end in -a, -e, -i and infinitives add -r (scribe 'writes', scriber 'to write').

## Articles

Definite **le**, indefinite **un**, both invariable. The prepositions a 'to' and de 'of' fuse with le into the compulsory contractions **al** and **del**. The definite article is used as in English, except that it is kept with titles before proper names and with abstract nouns standing for a whole class.

## Nouns

Nouns inflect for number only. Plural: **-s** after a vowel (catto → cattos), **-es** after a consonant (can → canes), **-hes** after -c to keep /k/ (roc → roches). Loanwords may keep foreign plurals (film → films, addenda, lieder).

No grammatical gender. Animate nouns are sex-neutral unless specifically male or female (jornalista neutral; rege/regina). Explicit feminines: substitute -a for final -o/-e (puero → puera) or add -essa (tigre → tigressa; duce → duchessa); from verbs with -atrice/-itrice (imperar → imperatrice; irregularly ager → actrice).

Nouns cannot serve as adjectives ('winter weather' → tempore hibernal; 'research laboratory' → laboratorio de recerca), except proper nouns (contator Geiger, motor Diesel, radios Röntgen). Apposition of equals is fine (arbore nano 'dwarf tree', nave domo 'house boat'); male and female forms should match.

## Adjectives

Free position — short adjectives tend to precede, long ones to follow; numerals always precede. No agreement ever; an adjective may take a plural ending only when the noun is absent (le parve infantes, but le parves 'the little ones').

Comparison: **plus/minus** + adjective; superlative **le plus/le minus**. Absolute superlative with **-issime** (excellentissime). Four adjectives have optional irregular forms:

| | comparative | superlative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bon 'good' | plus bon / melior | le plus bon / optime |
| mal 'bad' | plus mal / pejor | le plus mal / pessime |
| magne 'great' | plus magne / major | le plus magne / maxime |
| parve 'small' | plus parve / minor | le plus parve / minime |

## Adverbs

Primary adverbs are a closed class of grammatical operators (quasi 'almost', jam 'already', tovia 'anyway'). Secondary adverbs derive from adjectives with **-mente** (**-amente** after -c): felice → felicemente; magic → magicamente. A few have optional short forms in -o (solo/solmente). Comparison with plus/le plus as for adjectives; bon and mal have optional irregular adverbs (ben → melio → optimo; mal → pejo → pessimo).

## Pronouns

Personal pronouns inflect for number, case, and (third person) gender:

| | subject | object | after preposition | possessive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | io | me | me | mi(e) |
| 2 sg | tu | te | te | tu(e) |
| 3 sg m/f/n | ille / illa / illo | le / la / lo | ille / illa / illo | su(e) |
| 1 pl | nos | nos | nos | nostre |
| 2 pl | vos | vos | vos | vostre |
| 3 pl m/f/n | illes / illas / illos | les / las / los | illes / illas / illos | lor(e) |

- Object forms serve as direct and indirect objects (Dice me lo / Dice lo a me); third person has the longer forms after prepositions (Da le can a illes).
- Short possessives before bare nouns (mi amico), long ones elsewhere (le mie casa, le auto es le mie, amicos mie).
- **il** is impersonal (il pluve 'it's raining'; il es ver que...); **on** is the vague subject 'one/you/they' (oblique uno), often doing the work of a passive (On collige le recyclabiles omne venerdi).
- Reflexive **se** (third person), also for Romance-style intransitives and passives: Francese se parla in Francia.
- Vos is common as a formal singular; illes works as sex-neutral 'they'.
- Rare variants: yo/ego for io, il for ille, illi for illes (Occidental borrowings).

Demonstratives: adjective **iste** 'this' with pronouns iste/ista/isto (pluralizable); the remote demonstrative is **ille** 'that', whose pronouns match the third-person pronouns but are stressed in speech. iste encroaches on English 'that'; with two antecedents it refers to the latter.

Relatives and interrogatives: animate **qui** (subject and after prepositions) vs **que** (object); inanimates use que throughout; genitive **cuje** 'whose' for both; all replaceable by **le qual / le quales**.

## Verbs

A simplified English–Romance system: no imperfect/perfect/continuous aspects, no person inflection (except optionally for esser), and the indicative covers subjunctive and imperative. The main forms, on parlar 'speak', vider 'see', audir 'hear':

| form | ending | -ar | -er | -ir |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| infinitive | -r | parlar | vider | audir |
| present | — | parla | vide | audi |
| past | -va | parlava | videva | audiva |
| future | -ra | parlara | videra | audira |
| conditional | -rea | parlarea | viderea | audirea |
| present participle | -(e)nte | parlante | vidente | audiente |
| past participle | -te | parlate | vidite | audite |

Note the two asymmetries: -ir verbs take -iente (nutrir → nutriente), and -er verbs take past participle -ite (eder → edite).

### Simple tenses

- **Present**: infinitive minus -r; covers English simple and continuous present. esser, haber, vader normally take the short forms **es, ha, va**.
- **Past** in -va: covers simple past, past perfect, and their continuous forms.
- **Future** in -ra, stressed on the suffix (retornará).
- **Conditional** in -rea, also stressed on the suffix (prefereréa).

### Infinitives and participles

The infinitive covers English infinitive and gerund, and can even be pluralized (le faceres de illa 'her doings'). The present participle is an adjective or participial-phrase verb (un corvo parlante; approximante le station...). The past participle is an adjective and the compound-tense brick (un conto ben contate).

### Compound tenses

The three compound tenses mean exactly what the simple ones do:

- compound past: **ha** + past participle (Le imperio ha cadite = cadeva)
- compound future: **va** + infinitive (Io va retornar = retornara)
- compound conditional (rare): **velle** + infinitive (Io velle preferer... = prefererea)

The passive is **es** + past participle (Iste salsicias es fabricate per experte salsicieros). Complex tenses recombine the same bricks: future perfect habera finite, 'later she would write' vadeva scriber, passive past perfect habeva essite surveliate. A quasi-progressive va + present participle exists but is barely documented (Le climate va deveniente plus benigne 'keeps getting milder').

### Imperative and subjunctive

No distinct forms — the present tense serves, with a nominative pronoun after the verb for clarity (Face lo ora!; Va tu retro al campo). The infinitive is a more impersonal imperative (Cliccar hic). The cohortative uses a que-clause (Que illes mangia le brioche; Que nos resta hic); vamos 'let's' exists but some authorities deprecate it. **Sia** is the one true subjunctive/imperative form, of esser (Sia caute!; Que lor vita sia felice) — regular esse is also allowed.

### Irregular verbs

Only **es, ha, va** and **sia** are in general use. Collateral forms of esser — son (present plural), era (past), sera (future), serea (conditional) — have a significant minority of users (Que sera, sera); so, somos, vamos, van are rare. Official publications prefer the regular forms.

Some -er verbs have preferred present participles in -iente: caper, facer, saper, their compounds, and all verbs in -ciper, -ficer, -jicer, -spicer (facer → faciente beside regular facente; un homine sapiente). Most users today prefer the regular forms; sufficiente and friends live on as adjectives.

### Double-stem verbs

The Neo-Latin vocabulary brings hundreds of verbs whose stem mutates before certain suffixes: ager 'to act' has stems ag-/act- (agente, agentia but actrice, activista, reaction); sentir/sens-, repeller/repuls-. The twist: secondary-stem + -e yields an adjective equivalent to the past participle (experte ≈ experite 'experienced'), and Gode & Blair permitted the reverse — using scripte for scribite in compound tenses (Illa ha scripte con un pluma). This is controversial: a minority uses the recognizable ones (scripte), deprecators say it complicates active use, and a compromise reserves them for teaching the international scientific vocabulary.

## Numerals

Cardinals below 100 come from nineteen roots; smaller before larger multiplies, larger before smaller adds; tens and ones join with a hyphen (quaranta-duo). The roots: 0 zero, 1 un, 2 duo, 3 tres, 4 quatro, 5 cinque, 6 sex, 7 septe, 8 octo, 9 nove(m); 10 dece, 20 vinti, 30 trenta, 40 quaranta, then regularly 50 cinquanta … 90 novanta. Irregular teens: tredece, quattuordece, quindece, sedece. Hundred cento, thousand mille; larger numbers follow the long scale (million, milliardo, billion, billiardo…). The conjunction e may be inserted freely between roots (duo milles tres centos e quaranta-e-cinque).

Ordinals: prime, secunde, tertie, quarte, quinte, sexte, septime, octave, none, decime, ultime 'last'; all others take -esime after the cardinal (tres centesime), and in compounds only the last root changes (dece-prime, cento secunde). Fractions: medie 'half', otherwise cardinal + ordinal (vinti-duo septime '22/7'). Multiplicatives: duple/duplice, triple/triplice…, with prefixes uni-/mono-, bi-/di-, tri-, quadri-/tetra-… — all metric prefixes are productive, plus sesqui-, semi-, hemi-, myria-. Collectives take -ena (dozena 'dozen'). Conventions: decimal comma by ISO (3,1415), thousands separated by period or space; numeral abbreviations 1me, 2nde, 3tie, 4te… (adverbials in -o).

## Syntax

Normal order is SVO, relaxable when the sense is clear (Amandolos ama io tanto...). Pronoun objects follow the Romance SOV pattern — Ille los reface; Nos vole obtener lo — except with infinitives and imperatives (Jecta lo via!). With two pronoun objects, indirect comes first (Io les lo inviava per avion). Adverbs place as in English.

Questions, French-style: inversion (Ha ille arrivate?); an interrogative in subject position (Qui ha dicite isto?); the particle **esque** (or rarer an) for yes/no questions; or plain intonation with normal order (Tu jam ha finite?).
